# App review, October 2026

A full pass over the app: code bugs, screens and wording, how each health
assumption compares with published research, and what similar apps do. Fixes
marked **Fixed** are in the code; **Proposed** items wait for your OK.

## 1. Biology history (your request)

**Fixed.** Every Biology stat now opens a history view:

- 7 days / 30 days / 3 months / 6 months (only ranges with data).
- Bars for daily totals (steps, sleep, water, intensity minutes, energy),
  lines for measurements (heart rate, weight, VO₂ max, blood pressure).
- A dashed line at the average for the chosen range, so you see how the
  average is made up; goal lines (sleep 7 h, your step goal, protein) and a
  shaded typical range (blood oxygen 95-100%, breathing 12-20, BMI).
- Touch the chart to read a day; Average / Lowest / Highest / Days; a day-by-day
  list with each day's difference from the average.
- Sleep consistency shows each night as a bar from falling asleep to waking,
  with your average bedtime and wake time.
- Stats that had no history now have it: sleep, consistency, sleeping heart
  rate, intensity minutes, blood pressure (both numbers), weight (trend plus
  scale readings), protein.
- A plain "What it means" for each stat, with the source where a number comes
  from research.

## 2. Bugs and problems found

Three reviews read every file. The most important problems, all **fixed**:

### Data and scores
- Finishing a workout during a background refresh could leave it out of today's
  strain until the next refresh.
- Garmin data that synced more than a day late never counted toward strain for
  those days.
- Intensity minutes and the stress estimate counted only about half the minutes
  with Garmin, which records heart rate every 1-2 minutes.
- Calories from heart rate could count a single high reading as 10 minutes of
  exercise, and a workout left open overnight added ~2,000 kcal.
- Naps were merged into the night: a 1 pm nap made "wake time" 1:40 pm, which
  dropped the sleep score and broke sleep consistency.
- Sleep consistency broke for wake times near noon.
- One bad heart-rate reading could raise your learned max heart rate for a year.
- Protein's 7-day average included today's half-logged day, so it read "below
  target" most mornings.
- The coach saw different calorie numbers from the Home screen.
- Symptoms you reported never raised an illness alert on their own.

### Reminders, privacy, server
- Snoozed medication reminders were deleted the next time the app opened, and
  reminders stopped if the app wasn't opened for 3 days.
- Progress-photo analysis was on by default and, after a server error, re-sent
  your photos on every refresh.
- Anyone holding the app's public key could run unlimited AI calls on your
  Anthropic account. Now each user gets 60 coach messages and 3 photo
  analyses a day, checked on the server.
- The on-device coach forgot the conversation after every message.
- The Lock Screen rest timer could keep showing "Rest" after the countdown ended.
- The widget showed yesterday's strain and recovery after midnight.
- Progress photos were included in iCloud backups.

### Screens and wording
- White text on yellow/lime buttons was hard to read (Start workout, check-in
  buttons, Complete set, rest timer).
- Finishing a workout silently deleted sets you typed but didn't tick.
- Weights could mix kg and lb (steps and plate hints used the gym's unit).
- Body age said "Your habits are adding years" when you were younger.
- Home invented a strain target when there wasn't one; numbers were rounded
  differently on different screens; sleep score showed a "%".
- Evening check-in showed times (7 pm, 1 pm, 9 pm) it never saved.
- Text assumed Garmin and Cronometer everywhere, and jargon (HRV, RPE, Zone 2,
  AQI) wasn't explained.
- Muscle map showed recovered muscles grey while the legend said green.
- Recovery showed a full score before it had enough nights to be reliable.
- Onboarding couldn't scroll at large text sizes; several buttons were smaller
  than Apple's 44-point minimum.

## 3. Feature ideas (**all built**, October 2026)

From the research and competitor review (Whoop, Oura, Garmin, Apple Health,
Athlytic, Gentler Streak, Hevy, Fitbod, MacroFactor, Rise). Pick the numbers you
want.

| # | Feature | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Personal normal band** (30-60 day mean ± 1 SD) and a 7-day average line on every Biology chart, with each day coloured above/in/below | A single day is noisy; trends matter | Plews et al., Eur J Appl Physiol 2012; Sports Med 2013 |
| 2 | **Sleep debt** over the last 14 nights vs your need, with a "pay it back" plan | Explains tiredness after a week of short nights | Van Dongen et al., Sleep 2003; Watson et al., Sleep 2015 |
| 3 | **True Sleep Regularity Index** (minute-by-minute same-state probability) instead of bed/wake spread | It's the measure that predicted mortality | Windred et al., Sleep 2024 |
| 4 | **"What moved my recovery"**: each input's push in plain words | Trust and knowing what to change | Pattern from Garmin/Oura |
| 5 | **Caffeine cut-off time** from your bedtime (coffee ≥ 8.8 h before bed, pre-workout ≥ 13.2 h) | Easy fix for sleep | Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2023 |
| 6 | **Your alcohol effect**: your resting HR, HRV and sleep on drink vs no-drink nights | Personal, persuasive | Pietilä et al., JMIR Ment Health 2018; Gardiner et al., Sleep Med Rev 2025 |
| 7 | **Weekly hard sets per muscle** with indirect sets counted as half, target ~10-20 | Matches current volume research | Schoenfeld et al., J Sports Sci 2017; Pelland et al., Sports Med 2026 |
| 8 | **Rest-timer length by goal** (≥ 60-90 s for muscle, longer for strength) | Removes guesswork | Singer et al., Front Sports Act Living 2024 |
| 9 | **Lift progress charts** (estimated 1RM and PRs per exercise) | See progress | Strong / Hevy |
| 10 | **Protein per meal** (~0.4 g/kg, 4 meals) if Cronometer sends meal times | Simple daily habit | Schoenfeld & Aragon, JISSN 2018 |
| 11 | **Weight-loss speed guard**: warn above 1% of body weight a week; adjust targets weekly | Protects muscle | Helms et al., JISSN 2014 |
| 12 | **Step evidence update**: 7,000 a day as the floor, keep the adaptive goal | Newer, larger evidence | Ding et al., Lancet Public Health 2025 |
| 13 | **Cardio fitness card**: VO₂ max vs age bands; suggest 1-2 interval sessions a week when it stalls | Fitness is the strongest longevity marker | Mandsager et al., JAMA Netw Open 2018; Milanović et al., Sports Med 2015 |
| 14 | **Strength days for health**: weekly strength sessions with "2+ days" target | Linked with 10-17% lower mortality | Momma et al., Br J Sports Med 2022 |
| 15 | **Home blood pressure log** done properly (cuff readings, 7-day morning/evening average, AHA 2025 categories) | Clinically meaningful | 2025 AHA/ACC guideline |
| 16 | **Heat days**: lower strain target and pacing on hot days; count heat-acclimation days | Safety and realistic targets | Périard et al., Physiol Rev 2021 |
| 17 | **Morning light nudge** after sunrise, dim evenings | Cheap circadian help | Brown et al., PLoS Biol 2022 |
| 18 | **Illness watch: needs 2+ signals**, shows No / Minor / Major, and a "Sick / rest" mode that pauses targets | Fewer false alarms, kinder | Mishra et al., Nat Biomed Eng 2020; Quer et al., Nat Med 2021 |
| 19 | **Body age confidence** label and "what's moving it" | Honest about an estimate | Bevel / Whoop pattern |
| 20 | **Morning HRV with your phone camera** (1-minute reading) since Garmin doesn't share HRV | Recovery currently lacks HRV | Plews et al., IJSPP 2017 |
| 21 | **Lab results** (enter or upload a PDF; trends vs reference ranges; coach sees them) | Fills the blood-test gap | Whoop Advanced Labs, Bevel Health Records |
| 22 | **Sleep stages marked as estimates** and weighted lightly | Wrist staging is rough (Garmin kappa ~0.2) | Schyvens et al., Sleep Adv 2025 |

### Not worth copying
- Injury-risk scores from acute:chronic workload (Impellizzeri et al., IJSPP 2020).
- Blood pressure numbers from a watch or ring (AHA/ACC 2025 advises against).
- Glucose "spike scores" for people without diabetes (no outcome evidence).
- Exact deep/REM minutes from a wrist device.
- Zone 2 as the best way to raise VO₂ max (Storoschuk et al., Sports Med 2025).
- Rigid menstrual-phase training plans (McNulty et al., Sports Med 2020: trivial effects).
- A draining "body battery" score (no peer-reviewed validation found).

### Check on your phone first
Garmin Connect and Cronometer don't publish exactly what they send to Apple
Health. In the Health app → Browse, check whether these show "Garmin Connect" or
"Cronometer" as a source: HRV, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, VO₂ max, wrist
temperature, caffeine, alcohol, and whether meals have times.

## 4. Research check of the app's assumptions

Every number the app relies on was checked against the published research
(meta-analyses, position stands, guidelines).

### Wrong, now fixed
| What | Was | Now | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength estimate (1RM) | Ignored how many reps you had left, so planned weights came out too light | Counts reps left from your RPE | Zourdos et al., J Strength Cond Res 2016 |
| Body age: sleep | A group risk figure applied per missing hour | Applied once for under 6 h; long sleep flagged, not scored | Cappuccio et al., Sleep 2010 |
| Body age: steps | Kept adding credit past the point where benefit levels off | Stops at 10k (under 60) / 8k (60+) | Paluch et al., Lancet Public Health 2022 |
| Body age: BMI | Missing 18.5-20 and 40+ bands | Added | Global BMI Mortality Collaboration, Lancet 2016 |
| Blood pressure | "Elevated" at 130/80+ | Normal < 120/80, Elevated 120-129, High 130/80+ | ACC/AHA 2017 guideline |
| Vitamin D claim | "Lowered infection risk" | Evidence is mixed after the largest trials | Jolliffe et al., Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2025 |
| Zinc claim | Low diet intake "makes colds more likely" | The study was about zinc lozenges once a cold starts | Hemilä, JRSM Open 2017 |
| Vitamin C claim | "Shortens colds" from diet | Supplements helped mainly people under heavy physical stress | Hemilä & Chalker, Cochrane 2013 |
| Vitamin A limit | Flagged total vitamin A | The limit applies only to retinol, not vegetables | IOM DRI 2001 |
| "Low" nutrients | Under 70% of the RDA | Under the EAR (likely inadequate), with age bands | IOM, DRI Applications in Dietary Assessment 2000 |
| Check-in patterns | Any 5% difference with 3 days each side | 7+ days each side, bigger than your normal swing, t-test with false-discovery correction | Plews et al., Sports Med 2013 |
| Recovery baseline | 4 nights | 14 nights per signal | Javaloyes et al., IJSPP 2019 (4-week baseline) |
| Sleep need | Grew with yesterday's strain | 8 h by default; no study ties need to training load | Watson et al., Sleep 2015; Walsh et al., Br J Sports Med 2021 |
| Sleep efficiency | Full marks only at 100% | Full marks from 85% | Ohayon et al., Sleep Health 2017 |
| Deep + REM | 20% of sleep score | 10%: wrist devices stage sleep poorly | Chinoy et al., Sleep 2021; Schyvens et al., Sleep Adv 2025 |
| Lifting calories | 5 METs for hard lifting | 6.0 METs | 2024 Adult Compendium |
| Lifting strain | Used average set RPE when no workout rating | Uses the workout rating only (different scale) | Foster et al., J Strength Cond Res 2001 |
| VO₂ max labels | Made-up bands | FRIEND registry medians by age and sex | Kaminsky et al., Mayo Clin Proc 2015 |
| Heat advice | Outdated source, unverified "10+ bpm" | 2021 ACSM consensus | Roberts et al., Curr Sports Med Rep 2021 |
| Learned max heart rate | One 2-minute peak could stick for a year | Needs 3+ minutes inside a workout, within 15 bpm of the age estimate | Tanaka et al., J Am Coll Cardiol 2001 |

### Supported by research (kept)
Banister TRIMP constants; ACSM intensity cut-offs (40% / 60% of heart-rate
reserve); WHO 150 minutes a week; Tanaka max heart rate; the 7-day HRV trend vs
your normal range (Plews 2012, Javaloyes 2019); Mifflin-St Jeor; Keytel for
cardio; 10% for digestion; weight-loss 0.25-1% a week (Helms 2014); RPE scale
(Zourdos 2016); indirect sets as half (Pelland 2026); sodium/potassium (NASEM
2019); caffeine and alcohol effects (Gardiner 2023; Pietilä 2018); Gompertz
"effective age" (Spiegelhalter 2016).

### The app's own choices (no research exists, now labelled as such)
The 0-100 recovery score's weights and its 34/67 bands; strain's scale
(saturation and the RPE-to-load factor); the strain target bands; sleep-score
weights; body-fat years; muscle recovery hours; heart-rate zone edges; deload
rule. These are reasonable defaults, and the app and coach no longer present
them as validated.

### Follow-ups (**all built**)
1. **HRV that matches across devices.** Apple Watch gives SDNN, Garmin RMSSD.
   Computing RMSSD from Apple's beat-to-beat data during sleep only, with a
   separate baseline per source. (Garmin doesn't send HRV to Apple Health at
   all, so you'd still type it or use the camera reading in feature 20.)
2. **Protein when losing weight** based on lean mass or goal weight, so people
   with more body fat aren't told to eat too much protein (Helms 2014).
3. **Age-based step default** (8k under 60, 7k from 60) and 7,000 as the floor
   (Ding 2025).
4. **Rename "Stress (estimate)"** to "Heart rate above resting": no study
   validates heart rate alone as a stress score.
5. **True Sleep Regularity Index** (feature 3).
6. **Background Health updates** so the widget refreshes after a Garmin sync
   while the app is closed.
