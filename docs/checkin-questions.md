# Daily check-in questions (proposal, awaiting approval)

Two short forms: a morning one (about 30 seconds) and an evening one (about
45 seconds). Each question is here because research shows it changes HRV,
resting heart rate, sleep or how ready you are to train. After about 2-3 weeks
of answers, the app compares your own days, for example "after 2+ drinks your
HRV is 18% lower the next morning" (the same idea as Bevel's journal).

Answers are optional; skipping a question is fine.

## Morning (after waking)

| # | Question | Answer type | Why it matters (research) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | How rested do you feel? | 1-5 | Self-reported wellbeing tracks training stress as well as or better than blood markers or heart rate (Saw et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2016, systematic review of 56 studies). |
| M2 | How sore are your muscles? Where? | 0-10 + body areas | Soreness is one of the measures that responds most to training load (Saw 2016). Lets the trainer skip sore muscle groups. |
| M3 | Mood | 1-5 | Mood changes come before overtraining symptoms (Saw 2016; Meeusen et al., *Eur J Sport Sci* 2013 consensus on overtraining). |
| M4 | Feeling sick? (sore throat, congestion, fever, none) | Multi-select | Illness raises resting heart rate and lowers HRV, often a day before symptoms. Training hard while sick slows recovery (Mishra et al., *Nat Biomed Eng* 2020: wearable heart rate flagged COVID-19 early). |
| M5 | Did you wake up during the night to use the bathroom or for another reason? | None / 1 / 2+ | Explains sleep interruptions the watch sees; frequent waking lowers sleep quality independent of length. |
| M6 | Menstrual cycle (optional, hidden for male profile) | Period / not / don't track | Resting heart rate rises and HRV falls in the luteal phase (Schmalenberger et al., *J Clin Med* 2019, meta-analysis). Prevents "low recovery" false alarms. |

## Evening (before bed)

| # | Question | Answer type | Why it matters (research) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Caffeine today (mg), with quick picks: coffee 95, espresso 63, energy drink 80-160, pre-workout 150-300 | Number | 400 mg even 6 hours before bed cut sleep by over an hour (Drake et al., *J Clin Sleep Med* 2013). Caffeine's half-life is about 5 hours. |
| E2 | Time of your last caffeine | Time | Same study: timing matters as much as amount. Lets the app suggest a personal caffeine cutoff. |
| E3 | Alcoholic drinks today | Number | HRV and recovery drop with each drink, by about 9% after a low dose, 24% after moderate and 39% after high (Pietilä et al., *JMIR Ment Health* 2018, 4,098 people). Alcohol also suppresses REM sleep (Ebrahim et al., *Alcohol Clin Exp Res* 2013). |
| E4 | Time of your last drink | Time | Drinking closer to bed has a bigger effect on that night's HRV (Pietilä 2018). |
| E5 | Stress today | 1-5 | Mental stress lowers HRV and worsens sleep (Kim et al., *Psychiatry Investig* 2018, meta-analysis of HRV and stress). |
| E6 | Time of your last meal | Time | Eating close to bedtime was linked with worse sleep (Crispim et al., *J Clin Sleep Med* 2011). |
| E7 | Screens in bed (phone, TV) | None / under 30 min / 30+ min | Light-emitting screens before bed delay melatonin and make sleep later and lighter (Chang et al., *PNAS* 2015). |
| E8 | Time outdoors in daylight | None / under 30 min / 30+ min | Daylight helps set the body clock and is linked with better sleep (Burns et al., *J Affect Disord* 2021, UK Biobank, 400,000 people). |
| E9 | Hard exercise within 1 hour of bed? | Yes / no | Evening exercise doesn't harm sleep in general, except vigorous exercise ending within an hour of bed (Stutz et al., *Sports Med* 2019, meta-analysis). The app already knows workout times; this covers sessions it can't see. |
| E10 | Nicotine or vaping today | Yes / no | Nicotine raises heart rate and fragments sleep (Jaehne et al., *Sleep Med Rev* 2009). |
| E11 | Sauna or cold plunge | None / sauna / cold / both | Both change heart rate and blood pressure in the short term, and effects on recovery differ between people (Laukkanen et al., *Mayo Clin Proc* 2018 review). Worth tracking for your own pattern. |
| E12 | Took your medications? | Auto-filled from the Medications page | Only shown if a dose is unticked. |

## Filled in automatically (no question needed)

- Water and food: Cronometer (via Apple Health).
- Workouts, steps, sleep, heart rate: Garmin (via Apple Health).
- Doses taken: the Medications page.

## Left out on purpose

- **Sexual activity:** small, mixed effects on sleep in research; personal.
- **Supplements (beyond medications):** add them on the Medications page if you want reminders.
- **Exact sleep times:** your watch already records them.

## What the app does with the answers

1. **Today:** coaching uses this morning's answers, for example "you're sore in your legs and slept poorly: upper body today".
2. **Patterns:** after about 14 days, a "What affects you" section shows the factors linked with your HRV, resting heart rate and sleep score, ranked by size, with how many days it's based on.
3. **Personal limits:** for example, your caffeine cutoff time and the drink count where your recovery clearly drops.
