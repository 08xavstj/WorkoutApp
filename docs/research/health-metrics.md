# Health metrics: what matters, what to target, what Tensile should track

Research round, November 2026. Question: which health and fitness measures
matter most for living longer, feeling better and performing better; what the
evidence-based targets are; how each should be tailored to the user's goals
(lose / maintain / gain weight × build muscle / get stronger / stamina /
general health); and what Tensile should keep, add, change or drop.

How to read this:

- **Strength of evidence**: *strong* = meta-analyses of RCTs, large pooled
  cohorts with consistent dose-response, or guideline consensus; *moderate* =
  single large cohorts or small RCT meta-analyses; *weak* = small trials,
  single cohorts, mechanistic work; *mixed* = good studies that disagree.
- **Tensile today** was checked against the code (`Packages/AppCore`,
  `App/Services/HealthKitClient.swift`, `App/Features/*`) and README.md, not
  only the review document.
- **Effort**: small (a stat, a threshold, a line of coaching text), medium (a
  new card or check-in field with its own logic), large (a new subsystem).
- Every citation below was checked against a search result (title, authors,
  journal, year) except the handful marked **[not re-verified this round]**:
  the search budget ran out and PubMed is blocked from the sandbox. Those are
  well-known papers I am confident exist, but the numbers quoted for them
  should be checked before they go into app copy.
- Goal shorthand: **L** lose, **M** maintain, **G** gain weight; **Mu** muscle,
  **St** strength, **En** stamina/endurance, **He** general health.

## 1. Cardiorespiratory fitness and movement

| Measure | Evidence-based target or range | Why it matters (citation, strength) | Goal tailoring | Tensile today | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VO₂ max (cardiorespiratory fitness)** | Above the median for age/sex, and keep moving up: each +1 MET (≈3.5 mL/kg/min) is ≈11–17% lower all-cause mortality; lowest risk seen around 14 METs with no upper limit of benefit. For a man 20–40, "good" is roughly ≥45–50 mL/kg/min (FRIEND registry bands the app already uses). | Mandsager 2018 (122,007 patients; elite vs low fitness aHR 0.20; no ceiling), Kokkinos 2022 (750,302 veterans; HR 0.86 per MET; least fit 4× risk of most fit), Laukkanen 2022 meta-analysis (37 cohorts, 2.26 M people; RR 0.89 per MET), Lang 2024 umbrella review. **Strong** (observational but huge, graded, consistent). | Same target for every goal; it is the single most predictive number. **En**: make it the headline. **L**: cardio also helps the deficit, but do not let it eat recovery for lifting. **G/Mu/St**: 2 short sessions a week is enough to keep it from sliding; concurrent-training interference is small at that dose. | Tracked (HealthKit `vo2Max`), FRIEND age/sex bands, cardio-fitness card, feeds body age. Garmin may not export VO₂ max to Health: user should check. | **Keep.** Add a "fitness age" line computed only from VO₂ max (see section 8) and make the coach treat a flat or falling 90-day VO₂ max as a reason to add intervals. *Small.* |
| **Weekly moderate/vigorous minutes** | WHO: 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous per week (or mix), plus muscle work ≥2 days. Lowest mortality in the largest long-term cohort at 150–300 min vigorous or 300–600 min moderate; no extra benefit or harm above that. | Bull 2020 BJSM (WHO guideline), Lee 2022 Circulation (116,221 adults, 30 years). **Strong.** | **He/En**: aim for the upper band (300 min moderate-equivalent). **Mu/St/G**: the WHO floor (150 moderate-equivalent) is enough; more cardio is optional. **L**: push toward 300 min moderate, mostly walking, because it adds burn without hurting lifting. | Intensity minutes computed from heart rate (moderate + 2×vigorous), weekly goal 150. | **Change**: show a band (150 floor, 300 "best"), weight vigorous ×2 as now, and set the default by goal (He/En/L → 300, others → 150). *Small.* |
| **How to raise VO₂ max** | Both steady cardio and intervals work (≈+5 mL/kg/min over 2–3 months in untrained people). HIIT gives a small extra (+1.2 mL/kg/min). Long intervals (≥2 min), ≥15 min of work per session, ≥4 weeks beat moderate steady training; short low-volume HIIT is a time-efficient option. "Zone 2 is best for mitochondria" is not supported; higher intensities should be prioritised when training time is short. Polarised (lots easy + some very hard) beats threshold-heavy in small trials of trained athletes but the evidence is thin. | Milanović 2015 (28 trials), Wen 2019 (53 RCTs), Storoschuk 2025 narrative review (Sports Med), Rosenblat 2019 (4 trials, PEDro 4–5/10). **Strong** that both work; **moderate** for HIIT edge; **weak** for polarised superiority. | **En**: 1–2 long-interval sessions (4×4 min hard) + easy volume (polarised). **He/L**: 1 interval session + brisk walks. **Mu/St**: 1 short session of long intervals or a hard 20-min effort per week, placed after or far from leg day. | HR zones, strain, cardio training load. Review says "suggest 1–2 interval sessions when VO₂ max stalls" was built. | **Keep.** Coach copy should stop implying zone 2 is special. *Small.* |
| **Daily steps** | Mortality risk falls steeply to ≈5,000–7,000/day and flattens after; under 60 the curve keeps improving to 8,000–10,000. Even 4,000 is clearly better than 2,000. Every +1,000 ≈ −15% all-cause mortality near the bottom of the curve. | Paluch 2022 (15 cohorts, 47,471), Ding 2025 (57 studies, 35 cohorts; inflection ≈7,000), Banach 2023 (17 cohorts, 226,889). **Strong.** | **L**: steps are the cheapest extra burn; use 8–10k. **He**: 7–8k. **G/Mu/St**: 7k floor is fine; do not inflate. Older users: 6–8k. | Tracked, adaptive goal, 7k floor, age-based default (8k <60, 7k 60+), feeds body age. | **Keep.** *None.* |
| **Stepping pace / vigorous bursts** | A faster 30-min peak cadence lowers risk beyond total steps. Three to four 1–2 min vigorous bursts a day in non-exercisers (stairs, carrying shopping) ≈ 38–40% lower all-cause mortality in UK Biobank. | Paluch 2022 (peak-30 cadence HR 0.67), Stamatakis 2022 Nat Med (25,241 non-exercisers). **Moderate** (single large cohort). | Mostly relevant for people who do not train. A lifter already gets vigorous minutes. | Not tracked. | **Skip** for this user. Mention in coach copy only. |
| **Stairs** | Habitual stair climbing ≈ 24% lower all-cause and 39% lower CV mortality in a pooled analysis; dose unclear. | Paddock 2024, ESC Preventive Cardiology conference meta-analysis (5 studies, 455,649; I² 85%, conference abstract). **Weak–moderate** (not peer-reviewed as a paper). | A cheap nudge for **He/L**. Not a target. | `flightsClimbed` shown as a Biology stat. | **Keep** as a stat; no goal. *None.* |
| **Sitting time and breaks** | Long sitting is harmful mainly in people with little activity; ≈30–40 min/day of moderate-vigorous activity cancels the association. A 5-min walk every 30 min lowered post-meal glucose and systolic BP by 4–5 mmHg in a crossover trial; a 1-min walk every hour lowered BP but not glucose. | Ekelund 2020 BJSM (9 accelerometer cohorts, 44,370), Duran 2023 MSSE (randomised crossover). **Strong** for the first, **moderate** for break dosing. | Matters for desk workers. A lifter who hits 150+ vigorous minutes is largely protected; still useful for glucose on rest days. | Not tracked (`appleStandTime` not read; Garmin does not export stand hours to Health). | **Add (optional)**: a rest-day "move every 30 min" reminder toggle; no sitting score. *Small.* |

## 2. Muscle strength and mass

| Measure | Target or range | Why it matters | Goal tailoring | Tensile today | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Resistance training frequency and weekly minutes (for health)** | ≥2 days/week (WHO). Mortality curve is J-shaped: maximum ≈10–20% risk reduction at ≈30–60 min/week; ≈27% at ≈60 min in another pooled analysis; benefit fades (does not reverse clearly) above that. Combined with aerobic activity the association is larger (≈40% lower all-cause mortality vs neither). | Momma 2022 BJSM (16 cohorts), Shailendra 2022 AJPM (RR 0.85 any vs none), Bull 2020. **Strong** for "any vs none" and for 2 days/week; **moderate** for the J-shape (self-reported, few cohorts above 2 h/week). | The health dose is tiny; a lifter exceeds it. Do not tell a **Mu/St** user that 3+ hours is "too much": the J-curve is about mortality associations in general populations, not hypertrophy. Use the 30–60 min figure only for **He**-only users or during illness/deload ("even 2 short sessions keep the benefit"). | Strength days per week (target 2) built; session minutes recorded. | **Keep**; add a "health floor met" line (2 days and ≥30 min) so a deload week still reads as healthy. *Small.* |
| **Grip strength** | Each −5 kg ≈ +16% all-cause and +17% CV mortality, stronger than systolic BP. EWGSOP2 "probable sarcopenia" cut-offs: <27 kg men, <16 kg women. For a man in his 20s–40s a Jamar reading of ≈45–55 kg is average; aim above the age-band mean. | Leong 2015 Lancet (PURE, 139,691), Bohannon 2006 Physiotherapy reference values (12 sources, 3,317), Cruz-Jentoft 2019 EWGSOP2 cut-offs (applied in Tromsø 2020). **Strong** as a marker; **unknown** whether raising it changes risk. | For a lifter it is a free by-product of pulling; log it twice a year. Relative grip (kg per kg body weight) is used in cardiometabolic studies but I could not re-verify a cut-off this round. **L**: track absolute grip to confirm strength is kept during the deficit. | Not tracked. | **Add**: a manual "grip strength (kg)" lab-style entry with age/sex bands and the sarcopenia cut-off, measured with a dynamometer (cheap). *Small.* |
| **Chair rise / sit-to-stand** | Five rises >15 s = probable sarcopenia (EWGSOP2). 30-s chair-stand norms exist for 60+ (Rikli & Jones 1999). Low relative sit-to-stand *power* ≈ +57% mortality in older men (Toledo cohort 2025). | EWGSOP2 2019; Rikli & Jones 1999; Toledo 2025 (JCSM). **Moderate** in older adults; **not studied** in 20s–40s. | Only useful from ~55+ or during rehab. | Not tracked. | **Skip** for now; revisit if the app targets older users. |
| **10-s one-leg stand** | Failing to stand 10 s on one leg (3 tries) ≈ +84% all-cause mortality at 51–75. | Araujo 2022 BJSM (1,702 people, median 7 y). **Moderate** (single cohort, 51–75 only). | Irrelevant under 50; a yearly 20-second self-test from ~50. | Not tracked. | **Skip** until the user is 50+. |
| **Muscle mass / sarcopenia prevention** | Keep lifting; eat enough protein. Protein ≥1.6 g/kg/day in <65 (plateau ≈1.6, upper CI 2.2); 1.2–1.59 g/kg/day in ≥65 gives extra lean mass with lifting; without lifting protein alone does little. PROT-AGE: ≥1.0–1.2 g/kg for over-65s, ≥1.2 if active. | Nunes 2022 JCSM (74 RCTs), Morton 2018 (cited via Aragon 2022), Bauer 2013 PROT-AGE, Kirwan 2021 AJCN. **Strong.** | **G/Mu**: 1.6–2.2 g/kg. **L**: 2.3–3.1 g/kg of lean mass (Helms 2014) — the app's 2.6 g/kg LBM is inside that range. **M/He/En**: 1.2–1.6. Over 65: never below 1.2. | Protein target by goal; deficit uses lean mass; per-meal 0.4 g/kg; 7-day average. | **Keep.** Add an age switch (≥65 floor 1.2 g/kg). *Small.* |
| **Power training** | Power (fast concentric) training gives a small extra on physical function in older adults (SMD 0.30, low certainty). | Balachandran 2022 JAMA Netw Open (20 trials, 566), el Hadouchi 2022, Tschopp. **Moderate–weak.** | For **St** and anyone 50+, add a fast-concentric cue on a lift or two. Not a tracked measure. | Set/rep style by goal exists; no tempo cue. | **Add** a "lift fast on the way up" cue to the strength style for 50+. *Small.* |

## 3. Heart, blood pressure and blood markers

| Measure | Target or range | Why it matters | Goal tailoring | Tensile today | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Resting heart rate** | Lower is better across the range; ≈+16% mortality per +10 bpm after adjusting for fitness. <60 typical for trained people; a *rising* personal trend is the useful signal. | Jensen 2013 Heart (Copenhagen Male Study, 2,798 men, 16 y). **Moderate** as an independent marker (one cohort, <50% response); **strong** as a day-to-day recovery/illness signal (already used). | Same for all goals. Compare with own baseline, not population tables. | Tracked; baseline, recovery score, illness watch, body age. | **Keep.** *None.* |
| **Exercise heart-rate response and 1-min recovery** | Recovery ≤12 bpm in the first minute after a treadmill test = abnormal (adjusted RR 2.0 for death). A blunted *rise* during exercise (<89 bpm) ≈ 4× sudden death in middle-aged men. | Cole 1999 NEJM (2,428), Jouven 2005 NEJM (5,713 men, 23 y). **Strong** in lab tests; **weak** when taken from a wrist watch after a self-paced workout (not the same protocol). | Same for all goals; the trend is more informative than one value. | HRR after workouts (bands: <12 low, 12–20 fair, 20–30 good, 30+). | **Keep**, but label clearly that the 12-bpm cut-off comes from supervised maximal tests and watch values after an easy workout will read "low" for no reason: only use readings after hard sessions (peak ≥85% of max). *Small.* |
| **HRV (night-time RMSSD)** | No universal target; low SDNN ≈ +32–45% first CV event risk in people without known disease, roughly 1% lower risk per 1% higher SDNN. | Hillebrand 2013 Europace (8 studies, ≈22,000). **Moderate.** | Personal baseline and 7-day trend (HRV-guided training already in the app; no change). | Tracked with baseline, trend, HRV-guided target. | **Keep.** Note only. |
| **Blood pressure (home protocol)** | ACC/AHA 2017 and 2025: normal <120/80; elevated 120–129/<80; stage 1 130–139/80–89; stage 2 ≥140/90; treatment goal <130/80. ESC 2024: "elevated" 120–139/70–89, hypertension ≥140/90, treatment target 120–129 systolic. Home average over 7 days (≥12 readings) is the number that counts. | ACC/AHA 2017; AHA/ACC 2025 (Hypertension, DOI 10.1161/HYP.0000000000000249); ESC 2024. **Strong.** | Same for all goals. **G** on a surplus with creatine/sodium: re-check more often. Pre-workout stimulants raise readings; measure in the morning. | 7-day home protocol with 12-reading reliability flag and AHA categories. | **Keep.** Add a 3–6-monthly reminder to repeat the 7-day protocol (yearly if normal). *Small.* |
| **Lipids: LDL-C, non-HDL, ApoB** | Guidelines still treat LDL-C/non-HDL-C as the primary targets; ApoB is the better marker when they disagree (discordant high ApoB with low LDL-C: aHR 1.21 all-cause mortality). For primary prevention an LDL-C below ~100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) or ApoB <90 mg/dL is a common low-risk target; the risk relationship is continuous and lifetime exposure matters. | Copenhagen General Population Study (Johannesen 2021 JACC), ACC/AHA 2026 multisociety lipid guideline (as reported), ApoB discordance meta-analysis 2026. **Strong** for LDL/ApoB causality; **moderate** for ApoB over LDL-C in practice. | Same for all goals. **G**: a long surplus with high saturated fat raises ApoB; re-test after a bulk. Testosterone/anabolic use crushes HDL and raises ApoB: the app's testosterone marker should link to this. | LDL, HDL, total, TG, ApoB in `LabMarkers` with ranges; PDF import. | **Keep.** Change the ApoB "about" text to say it is the better single number and the one to watch if LDL-C looks fine. Frequency: every 1–2 years if normal, 3 months after a diet change. *Small.* |
| **Lp(a)** | Measure **once** in adulthood; genetically fixed; >50 mg/dL (≈125 nmol/L) is high and should intensify control of every other risk factor. | Kronenberg 2022 Eur Heart J (EAS consensus). **Strong** (causal, genetic). | Same for all goals. | Not in `LabMarkers`. | **Add** Lp(a) with the "once in a lifetime" note and nmol/L + mg/dL units. *Small.* |
| **HbA1c and fasting glucose** | Prediabetes: HbA1c 5.7–6.4% or fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL (5.6–6.9 mmol/L); diabetes ≥6.5% / ≥126. | ADA Standards of Care 2024. **Strong.** | **L** with excess fat: HbA1c is the number most likely to improve. For lean lifters a slightly high fasting glucose after a high-protein breakfast or hard training is noise: test fasted and rested. | Both in `LabMarkers`. | **Keep.** Yearly if normal. *None.* |
| **hs-CRP** | <1 mg/L low, 1–3 average, >3 high CV risk; it is also raised for days by a cold, a hard session or a tattoo, so repeat if >3. | Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration 2010 Lancet **[not re-verified this round]**; Ridker's JUPITER used ≥2 mg/L. **Moderate** as a risk marker; **weak** as something to act on alone. | Same for all goals; test when rested. | In `LabMarkers` with the right bands and the "hard workout raises it" caveat. | **Keep.** *None.* |
| **Ferritin / iron** | Low ferritin (commonly <30 ng/mL, lower thresholds in some labs) impairs endurance performance and recovery even without anaemia; endurance athletes and menstruating women are the at-risk groups. **[athlete threshold not re-verified this round]** | Sports-medicine consensus (e.g. Sim 2019 review). **Moderate.** | **En** and female users: check yearly or when tired/stalling. Lifters rarely need it. | Ferritin, haemoglobin, haematocrit in `LabMarkers`. | **Keep.** Consider a "low-normal (30–50) may still matter for endurance" note. *Small.* |
| **Vitamin D (25-OH)** | Deficiency (<20 ng/mL / 50 nmol/L) is worth correcting; supplementing replete people does not lower mortality, CV events or cancer incidence (RR 0.98 all-cause); a 16% lower *cancer death* signal exists, mainly with D3. | VITAL 2019 NEJM (25,871), Zhang 2019 BMJ (52 trials, 75,454). **Strong** that routine supplementation does little; **moderate** that deficiency is worth fixing. | Same for all goals; indoor lifters at high latitude in winter are the ones likely to be low. | In `LabMarkers`; dietary vitamin D read. The review already removed the "fewer infections" claim. | **Keep.** Test once in winter; supplement only if low. *None.* |
| **Testosterone** | Normal-range differences do not predict muscle gain in healthy men. Supraphysiological doses raise fat-free mass dose-dependently (Bhasin 2001); replacement in hypogonadal men with CV risk did not raise CV events (TRAVERSE) but increased fractures and the result does not apply to men without a diagnosis. Measure in the morning, fasted, twice, only if symptoms. | Bhasin 2001 AJP Endocrinol Metab; Lincoff 2023 NEJM (TRAVERSE, 5,246). **Strong** for both; the "optimise T for gains" idea is **hype**. | Same for all goals. If the user is on TRT, lipids, haematocrit and BP need closer watching (the app's medications module can note this). | Total testosterone in `LabMarkers`. | **Change** the "about" text: low-normal is not a performance problem; sleep, deficit size and alcohol move it; two morning samples before any conclusion. *Small.* |
| **"Cardiovascular age" / heart age** | Heart-age calculators are communication devices built on risk equations; there is no evidence they motivate change better than the plain risk percentage, and they are not validated as predictions of their own. | Bonner 2021 JMIR Cardio systematic review; Bonner 2015 (patient experience). **Weak** as a motivator; **not validated** as an outcome. | Not goal-specific. | Not shown (body age is separate). | **Do not add** a heart age. If lipids, BP and HbA1c are all present, show the PREVENT 10-year risk percentage instead (the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline's equation) with "an estimate for groups, not you". *Medium.* |

## 4. Body composition

| Measure | Target or range | Why it matters | Goal tailoring | Tensile today | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Waist-to-height ratio (WHtR)** | Keep waist < half your height (WHtR < 0.5); 0.5–0.6 raised, >0.6 high. Predicts diabetes, CVD and all outcomes better than BMI and slightly better than waist alone. | Ashwell 2012 Obesity Reviews (meta-analysis); Browning 2010 (the 0.5 boundary). **Strong.** | Best single body-composition number for every goal because it ignores muscle mass. **G**: the guard rail for a bulk (stop gaining when WHtR crosses 0.5–0.52). **L**: the progress number when the scale stalls. | Not tracked (HealthKit `waistCircumference` not read). | **Add**: read/write waist circumference, show WHtR with the 0.5 line, monthly reminder; feed it to body age *instead of* BMI when both exist. *Medium.* |
| **BMI** | 20–25 lowest mortality in never-smokers; wrong for muscular people. | Global BMI Mortality Collaboration 2016 Lancet **[cited in the app already; not re-verified this round]**. **Strong** in populations, **weak** for a lifter. | Treat as context only. | Tracked; body age uses it. | **Change**: when body fat or WHtR exists, drop BMI from body age; keep the stat. *Small.* |
| **Body-fat %** | Gallagher 2000 healthy ranges for 20–39: men ≈8–20%, women ≈21–33% (41–59: 11–22% / 23–34%), derived from BMI equivalents, not outcomes. | Gallagher 2000 AJCN (1,626 adults, 4-compartment/DXA). **Moderate** (no mortality link). | **L**: aim for the lower half; **G/Mu**: stop a bulk at ~18–20% (men) and recomp; **St**: higher is tolerated. | Tracked via HealthKit (scale) and used for Katch–McArdle and body age ("body-fat years" are an app choice). | **Keep**, but show the error bar (next row). *Small.* |
| **How the number is measured: bioimpedance scales, wrist BIA, photos** | Consumer BIA has small average bias but individual limits of agreement of several % fat (worse at high fat); only trends under constant conditions (morning, fasted, after toilet) mean anything. The best published 2-photo model reached MAE ≈2.2% and limits ±5% against DXA, in tight clothing, standardised pose, funded by the vendor. Weekly photo changes smaller than that are noise. | Foot-to-foot BIA validations (2003, 2006, 2018, 2025, 2026 studies, consistently "fine for groups, not individuals"); Majmudar 2022 npj Digit Med (134 adults, Amazon-funded). **Strong** that single readings are unreliable. | Same for all goals. | Scale body fat shown as a value; weekly Claude photo analysis. | **Change**: show body fat as a 14-day trend with "±3–4% per reading"; tell the photo analysis to report only direction over ≥4 weeks and never a number. *Small.* |
| **Weight-change speed** | Lose 0.5–1% of body weight per week (0.7%/week kept or gained lean mass in athletes; 1.4%/week did not, short term; no difference at 12 months). Gain 0.25–0.5%/week. | Garthe 2011 IJSNEM (24 athletes), Helms 2014 JISSN. **Moderate.** | **L+Mu** (recomp): 0.5%/week; **L** only: up to 1%; **G**: 0.25–0.5%. | Weight-speed guard at 1%/week, rate options per goal (lose 0.25–1.0, gain 0.25–0.5). | **Keep.** *None.* |
| **Lean-mass preservation in a deficit** | Resistance training + ≥1.6 g/kg protein (2.3–3.1 g/kg lean mass in lean people) + ≥7 h sleep: in a 14-day crossover, 5.5 h sleep cut fat loss by 55% and raised lean loss 60% vs 8.5 h. | Nedeltcheva 2010 Ann Intern Med (n=10, crossover), Nunes 2022, Helms 2014. **Strong** for protein and lifting; **weak-moderate** for the sleep number (tiny trial) but consistent with larger sleep-restriction work. | **L**: this is the whole plan. **G**: not applicable. | Protein and sleep debt exist; not linked. | **Add** a coaching rule: in a deficit, if sleep debt >5 h over 14 nights, say the deficit is costing muscle and ease the rate. *Small.* |

## 5. Sleep

| Measure | Target or range | Why it matters | Goal tailoring | Tensile today | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | ≥7 h per night for adults 18–60 (AASM/SRS); NSF range 7–9 h; ≤6 h is inadequate; >9 h is fine for young adults and when paying back debt. | Watson 2015 (AASM/SRS consensus), Hirshkowitz 2015 Sleep Health. **Strong.** | **Mu/G**: aim 8 h (anabolic and appetite reasons). **L**: ≥7 h is the floor that protects lean mass. **En**: 8+ around big weeks. | Sleep score vs 8-h need, sleep debt (14 nights), long sleep flagged not scored. | **Keep.** *None.* |
| **Regularity (SRI)** | Regular timing predicts mortality better than duration; top four SRI quintiles had 20–48% lower all-cause mortality than the least regular. Irregular sleepers have later clocks and worse outcomes even at equal duration. | Windred 2024 Sleep (60,977 UK Biobank, actigraphy), Phillips 2017 Sci Rep (61 students; SRI correlates with grades, r=0.37). **Strong** association; causality unproven. | Same for all goals; shift workers get a different message. | True SRI built. | **Keep.** Promote SRI to the Sleep card headline alongside duration. *Small.* |
| **Timing and chronotype** | Evening types show modestly higher morbidity/mortality in UK Biobank **[Knutson & von Schantz 2018, not re-verified this round]**; social jet-lag (weekday/weekend midpoint shift >1–2 h) is linked to cardiometabolic risk. The fix is the same as regularity: anchor wake time, morning light. | **Moderate.** | Same for all goals. | Average bedtime/wake time shown. | **Add** a "weekend shift" number (weekday vs weekend sleep midpoint) with a 1-h target. *Small.* |
| **Sleep apnoea screening** | STOP-Bang ≥3: sensitivity ≈85–95% for moderate-severe OSA, specificity ≈30–47% (good at ruling out, poor at ruling in). Apple Watch notification: ≈66% sensitivity, ≈98.5% specificity for AHI ≥15; no notification does not mean no apnoea. Big-necked lifters, snorers and people with morning headaches or resistant BP are the target group. | Chung group meta-analyses (BMC Anesthesiol 2022; Sleep Breath 2021), FDA 510(k) K240929. **Strong** for STOP-Bang characteristics. | Same for all goals; **G** users gaining neck size are more at risk. | No screening. Overnight SpO₂ and respiration are tracked. | **Add** a one-time STOP-Bang (8 yes/no questions) in Biology with "≥3: ask for a sleep study", re-asked yearly or when BP becomes elevated; keep the overnight SpO₂ history next to it. *Medium.* |
| **Naps** | Naps <30–60 min are neutral or helpful; habitual naps ≥60 min ≈ +22% mortality and higher CV risk in cohorts (reverse causation likely). | 2024 meta-analysis (21 cohorts, ≈374,000); Pan 2020 ESC (20 cohorts); 2015 dose-response. **Moderate**, observational. | **En/Mu** after short nights: a 20–30 min nap is fine and helps. Flag naps >60 min as a sign of sleep debt or illness, not as a bad habit. | Naps separated from the night (fixed in the review). | **Keep**; add the "short naps fine, long naps = pay back debt" copy. *Small.* |
| **Caffeine** | Done (Gardiner 2023 cut-offs in the app). | — | — | Built. | Keep. |
| **Light exposure** | More daytime light and less night light are each linked to lower risk of depression (≈−20% / +30%), psychosis and other disorders in 86,772 UK Biobank people; cross-sectional. Daylight sets the clock (Phillips 2017: regular sleepers got brighter, earlier light). | Burns 2023 Nature Mental Health. **Moderate** (observational, single cohort). | Same for all goals; indoor lifters in winter are the use case. | Not tracked (HealthKit `timeInDaylight` not read; the proposed evening question E8 was not built; weather model knows sunrise). | **Add**: read `timeInDaylight` (Apple Watch only; Garmin no) and a "get outside in the first 2 h after waking" nudge keyed to sunrise. *Small–medium.* |
| **Bedroom temperature** | Sleep efficiency was best at 20–25 °C and fell 5–10% from 25 → 30 °C in older adults; large individual differences. | Baniassadi 2023 Sci Total Environ (50 adults, 18 months). **Weak–moderate** (older adults only). | Not goal-specific. | Outdoor temperature known (weather). | **Skip** (no indoor sensor; cannot act on it). Mention heat-wave nights in the sleep card copy. *None.* |
| **Sleep and fat loss / muscle gain** | See section 4: short sleep in a deficit shifts loss from fat to lean. | Nedeltcheva 2010. | Rule for **L**. | Not linked. | Add (section 4). |
| **Insomnia: CBT-I** | First-line treatment for chronic insomnia (AASM); digital/self-help CBT-I has medium-to-large effects and is a valid alternative when a therapist is not available. | AASM guidance; Simon 2023 Sci Rep network meta-analysis; JAMA Intern Med meta-analysis (67 RCTs, g≈0.98). **Strong.** | Not goal-specific. | Nothing. | **Add** a one-line referral: if sleep-onset >30 min or efficiency <85% on most nights for a month, suggest CBT-I (apps or clinician) rather than more sleep tips. ## 6. Mental health and stress

## The 15 highest-payoff measures or nudges for a lifter in his 20s–40s

Ordered by (expected health payoff) × (how actionable it is in this app).

1. **VO₂ max, trend over 90 days, with a fitness-age line.** Strongest single predictor of lifespan (Mandsager, Kokkinos, Laukkanen); the lever is 1–2 long-interval sessions a week (Wen 2019). Already tracked; make it the headline of the Fitness tab. *Small.*
2. **Weekly moderate/vigorous minutes with a 150-floor / 300-best band**, default by goal. (WHO 2020; Lee 2022.) *Small.*
3. **Strength training ≥2 days and ≥30–60 min a week as a "health floor"** that a deload still satisfies. (Momma 2022; Shailendra 2022.) *Small.*
4. **Sleep regularity (SRI) promoted next to duration**, plus a weekend-shift number. (Windred 2024; Phillips 2017.) *Small.*
5. **Waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio** with the 0.5 line, monthly reminder, and WHtR replacing BMI in body age. The best body-composition number for someone with muscle. (Ashwell 2012.) *Medium.*
6. **Home blood pressure, repeated every 3–6 months** with a reminder, ACC/AHA 2025 categories. (Already built; add the reminder.) *Small.*
7. **ApoB (plus LDL-C, non-HDL) every 1–2 years and after a bulk**, with the ApoB copy upgraded. (Copenhagen discordance analysis; guidelines.) *Small.*
8. **Lp(a) once.** New lab marker. (Kronenberg 2022.) *Small.*
9. **Steps, 7k floor, 8–10k when losing weight.** Already built. *None.*
10. **Protein by goal and age** (1.6–2.2 g/kg; 2.3–3.1 g/kg lean mass in a deficit; ≥1.2 g/kg from 65). Already built; add the age floor. (Nunes 2022; Helms 2014; PROT-AGE.) *Small.*
11. **Sleep-debt × deficit rule**: short sleep in a cut costs muscle; ease the deficit when 14-night debt is high. (Nedeltcheva 2010.) *Small.*
12. **Cold-plunge warning on lifting days** and a sauna "2–4×/week, 15–20 min" dose line. (Roberts 2015; Piñero 2024; Laukkanen 2015.) *Small.*
13. **Alcohol weekly total against the Canadian tiers**, keeping the personal HRV effect that is already built. (CCSA 2023; GBD 2022.) *Small.*
14. **Grip strength as a twice-yearly manual entry** with age bands and the 27-kg sarcopenia line. (Leong 2015; Bohannon 2006; EWGSOP2.) *Small.*
15. **STOP-Bang once + yearly**, next to overnight SpO₂, with the Apple sleep-apnoea notification caveat. (Chung meta-analyses; FDA K240929.) *Medium.*

Close behind: fibre target 25–29 g (small, cheap fix to an existing stat); headphone exposure vs the WHO 80 dB/40 h allowance (small); morning-daylight nudge keyed to sunrise (small); monthly PHQ-2/GAD-2 (small–medium); a preventive-care checklist with dates (medium).

## What to drop or de-emphasise (evidence weak, not actionable, or not measurable here)

- **Heart-age / cardiovascular-age numbers.** No validation as predictions; no behaviour-change advantage over a plain risk percentage (Bonner 2021). Show the PREVENT percentage if labs allow, or nothing.
- **DNA-methylation "biological age" from a mail-in kit.** Good at population level (Levine 2018), noisy for one person over months; the app has no way to measure it. Do not build around it.
- **Body-fat % to one decimal, from a scale or a photo.** Individual error of several percent (BIA validations; Majmudar 2022). Show 14-day trends with a ±3–4% note; have the photo analysis report direction, not numbers.
- **BMI inside body age** once waist or body fat exist. Wrong for muscular people (Global BMI Collaboration numbers are for the general population).
- **Zone-2-as-the-key-to-mitochondria coaching.** Not supported (Storoschuk 2025); HIIT and continuous both raise VO₂ max (Milanović 2015; Wen 2019).
- **Heart-rate recovery from easy watch workouts.** The 12-bpm cut-off is from supervised maximal tests (Cole 1999); keep HRR but only from hard sessions, and never as a risk flag.
- **Chair rise, one-leg stand, power training, falls work for a user under 50.** Real evidence, wrong age group (Araujo 2022 is 51–75; EWGSOP2 is older adults). Revisit at 50.
- **Testosterone optimisation.** Normal-range levels do not predict gains; TRAVERSE safety does not apply without a diagnosis; two morning samples before any conclusion.
- **Vitamin D as a performance or immunity lever.** Supplement only if deficient (VITAL; Zhang 2019).
- **Journaling, gratitude, time-in-nature and social-connection scores.** Effects are small (Frattaroli 2006) or the data cannot be collected honestly by a fitness app (White 2019, Holt-Lunstad 2010 are population associations). One line of coach copy at most.
- **Posture, screen-time, eye-health and bedroom-temperature metrics.** Weak evidence or no sensor.
- **Ultra-processed-food tracking.** Strong-ish association (Lane 2024) but Cronometer does not send NOVA classes; nothing to compute.
- **Daily "stress score" from heart rate.** Already renamed; keep it renamed. The 1–5 stress item is for trends only; PHQ-2/GAD-2 monthly is the validated screen.
- **Breathwork or mindfulness scores.** Offer a timer, never a score (Fincham 2023: small effects, moderate bias).
- **A 30–60 min/week "cap" on lifting.** The J-curve in Momma 2022 is a mortality association in general populations, not a reason for a lifter to train less.
