# Competitors, November 2026: what the best apps do better than Tensile, what users hate, and what to build next

9 November 2026. This is a follow-up to `competitors.md` (3 Nov 2026). That file covers the broad feature matrix for health, sleep, labs and nutrition. This one focuses on **lifting and the Watch**, on **user complaints**, and on what changed in Apple's own apps (watchOS 26.4/27, the iOS 27 Health app). It also updates the user profile: he now wears an **Apple Watch Ultra** (not Garmin), uses AirPods Pro 3 and Cronometer, and wants to lose fat, build muscle and get stronger.

**How this was researched.** Web search only. In this sandbox WebFetch could not resolve most product sites (hevyapp.com, macrofactor.com, sensai.fit, macstories.net), so facts come from search-result summaries of official pages, help centres, App Store listings, press coverage and review sites. Many 2026 "review" sites are affiliate pages or competitors' blogs (SensAI, Alpha Progression, Dr. Muscle, JEFIT, Setgraph and others review their rivals). Claims that only appear on those sites are marked **(secondary)**. Prices change often and the sources disagree, so treat every price as approximate. I found little first-hand Reddit text; the complaint patterns below come from Reddit snapshots, Trustpilot, vendor feedback boards (Bevel's Canny), Whoop's community forum and MacRumors threads.

**What Tensile has today** (checked in `App/`, `Watch/`, `Packages/AppCore` on 9 Nov 2026): equipment-aware planner with plate-exact loads (`WorkoutPlanner`, `Equipment`), adaptive week (`AdaptiveWeek`), training blocks (`TrainingBlock`), double progression and RIR autoregulation (`Autoregulation`, `SetFeedback`), set kinds (drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps, to failure: `SetKinds`), supersets, warm-up ramp and dynamic warm-up (`WarmUp`, `WarmUpRoutine`), pain log (`PainGuard`), lift goals with projections, abs finisher, workout summary, easy cardio planner, muscle map and weekly sets, recovery-aware targets, Watch app (set logging with an effort step, rest timer, rep counter, complications), Live Activity, HealthKit workout with effort score, an on-device coach that returns startable workouts, and a large health side (HRV/RHR/VO₂ max with estimates, BP, labs, vitamins, supplements, medications, Cronometer nutrition). Exercise guides use free-exercise-db photos **fetched from GitHub at runtime** and a "Watch form videos" link to a YouTube search. Sharing is a text `ShareLink` of the workout summary. No data export or import, no Siri/App Shortcuts, and the Watch cannot start a planned session without the iPhone ("No workout on your iPhone").

## 2. What users complain about (avoid these)

1. **Too many taps while lifting.** The most repeated logging complaint (Setgraph and GymGrid summaries of Reddit; a Trainerize request about supersets taking a tap per exercise). Apple itself had to ship watchOS 26.4 to restore one-tap workout start after an r/AppleWatch backlash ("worse in every way"). *Tensile risk:* the effort sheet before each working set on the Watch.
2. **Not remembering last time / retyping.** Users leave apps that don't pre-fill from the previous session. Bevel added "pre-fill weights from last workout" and "auto-update template". *Tensile:* has live targets from history; keep it that way everywhere, including the Watch and empty workouts.
3. **Opaque or random programming.** Fitbod's "algorithm is broken", "dangerous weights", repetitive picks; Dr. Muscle rotates exercises too often. Lifters want to know *why* a weight was chosen and want the same core lifts to stay put long enough to progress. *Tensile:* keep main lifts stable within a block; show the reason on every target.
4. **Sync failures and lost data.** Bevel's board: "never syncs", "lost all history", Watch widget numbers differ from the phone. JEFIT: data loss. Whoop: a "bug" made HR wrong for a long time. *Tensile:* complications must show the same number as the phone (or "--"), and the user needs an export/backup.
5. **Wrist heart rate during lifting is unreliable,** so HR-only strain undercounts lifting (Whoop forum thread across hardware generations). *Tensile already handles this* with max(HR TRIMP, sRPE).
6. **AI that invents things.** Bevel's AI suggested exercises the app doesn't have and failed to build the template. *Tensile:* the coach must only return exercises from the library that the gym supports (the planner rule in CLAUDE.md applies to coach output too).
7. **Paywalls and subscription fatigue.** Fitbod and Dr. Muscle billing complaints, Whoop's subscription-only device, a class action over upgrade promises; Hevy's and Liftosaur's caps on routines, graphs and Watch. *Tensile:* no paywalled basics; if it ever charges, a lifetime option and a free core.
8. **Bloat.** Users who want stats don't want feeds, challenges and marketplaces (Unstar's analysis of negative reviews in the top 50 health apps; JEFIT's clutter). *Tensile:* the five tabs are already dense; new features should mostly live inside existing cards.
9. **Rest timers that don't notify or reset when backgrounded.** A recurring Reddit gripe. *Tensile:* has a Live Activity and rest alerts; test it with the phone locked and the Watch off.
10. **Tedious manual entry for wearables' strength features.** Whoop Strength Trainer "tedious to fill in". *Tensile:* rep counter and pre-filled targets help; don't add required fields.
11. **Generic first weeks.** Fitbod needs 10–15 sessions to personalise. *Tensile:* estimated starting weights already exist; ask two or three anchor lifts at onboarding to make week one right.

## 3. Recommendations for Tensile

Ordered by value for this user (Ultra wearer, lifting 3–5×/week, wants fat loss + muscle + strength) and by how often users ask for it. Effort: **S** ≈ a day or two, **M** ≈ a week, **L** ≈ several weeks.

### Quick wins

**1. One-tap set logging on the Watch (and phone).** *What:* make "Done as planned" the default action for a working set: one tap logs the target reps and weight and starts rest. Ask "how many in the tank?" only on the last set of an exercise, when the user changes reps, or as a Digital Crown scroll on the rest screen (optional, prefilled with the target RIR). *Why:* logging speed is the top complaint across lifting apps; Strong's Watch app is loved for exactly this; Apple had to restore one-tap start in 26.4. RIR on the final set is enough for autoregulation, which progresses on the last-set RIR in most published protocols (Helms et al. 2016 RIR-based RPE scale; Zourdos et al. 2016). *Where:* `Watch/WatchEffortSheets.swift`, `Watch/WatchLiveView.swift`, `ActiveWorkoutView`, `Autoregulation` (accept missing per-set RIR). **S–M.**

**2. Bundle exercise images offline and show one cue on the live set.** *What:* ship the free-exercise-db images (public domain) in the app or download once on first launch and cache; show the first-frame image and one short cue under the exercise name in the live workout and on the Watch. *Why:* images currently load from raw.githubusercontent.com at runtime, which fails in basement gyms; every leading app shows demos offline. *Where:* `App/Services/ExerciseLibrary.swift`, `ExerciseGuide.swift`, `ActiveWorkoutView`. **S** (watch the app size; download-and-cache keeps it small).

**3. Show why each target is what it is.** *What:* a small "why" line or tap on the target: "+2.5 kg: you hit 10 reps on every set at 2 in the tank last time", "−1 set: recovery is an easy day", "Held: shoulder niggle in your pain log". *Why:* the main complaint about Fitbod and Dr. Muscle is opaque, random-feeling progression; Juggernaut and RP keep users by explaining. Fits CLAUDE.md's "explain the evidence" rule. *Where:* `SessionTargets`, `SetFeedback` (return a reason enum), live set row, Watch set screen (short form). **S–M.**

**4. Export and backup; import from Strong/Hevy.** *What:* Settings › Data: export all sets as CSV (Strong-compatible columns) and a full JSON backup; import Strong and Hevy CSV exports to seed lift history and e1RM. *Why:* lost-data complaints (Bevel, JEFIT), lock-in fears in Reddit advice ("check data export before committing"), and import makes the planner's first weeks right for anyone switching. *Where:* new `DataExport` service, `SettingsView`; parsing in AppCore with tests. **S** (export) + **M** (import).

**5. App Shortcuts, Siri and the Ultra's Action button.** *What:* App Intents for "Start today's workout", "Log set as planned", "Start rest", "What's my recovery?", "Next session"; on watchOS, an Action-button start for the planned session. *Why:* this user has an Ultra; the Action button is the fastest way to start or log; Siri questions are on the README roadmap; App Intents also surface in Spotlight and Smart Stack. *Where:* new `Shared/Intents`, Watch target. **S–M.**

**6. Shareable workout card image.** *What:* render the summary (time, volume, PRs, muscle map, strain added) as an image with `ImageRenderer` for Messages/Instagram; keep the text option. No feed, no followers. *Why:* sharing is Hevy's main growth loop and a motivation for many; a private-by-default card fits Tensile's privacy stance. *Where:* `WorkoutSummaryView`, `App/Design/SummaryCard.swift`. **S.**

**7. Say clearly how Tensile differs from Apple's new Readiness and Health Age.** *What:* when iOS 27.3 ships, add one line in the Recovery and Body age explainers: what Tensile uses (lifting load, muscle recovery, sleeping HR, 7-day HRV band), where its number can differ from Apple's, and that both are estimates. Don't add a second score or try to match Apple's. *Why:* users with both will see two readiness numbers and ask which is right; "inaccurate tracking" is the top complaint category (Unstar). *Where:* `RecoveryContributorsSheet`, `EvidenceDisclosure`, `BodyAge` copy. **S.**

### Bigger features

**8. Start and run the planned session on the Watch without the iPhone.** *What:* sync the week's plan (exercises, targets, plates, warm-ups) to the Watch ahead of time so that "Start Push" works with the phone in a locker; log offline and reconcile later (the queue already exists for unplanned workouts). *Why:* Liftosaur and MacroFactor now offer phone-free Watch workouts; Workout Buddy works without the phone in watchOS 27; Ultra owners often leave the phone behind. Today the Watch says "No workout on your iPhone". *Where:* `WatchSync`, `Shared/WatchSession`, `Watch/WatchStartView.swift`, `WatchWorkoutModel`; progression maths stays on the phone, applied when it syncs. **M–L.**

**9. Post-session muscle feedback that steers volume.** *What:* at Finish, an optional 3-tap card per trained muscle: pump (low/ok/great), soreness from last time (none/healed/still sore), performance (down/same/up). Feed `VolumeProgression` and `TrainingBlock` (add a set when feedback is good, hold or cut when still sore or performance fell). *Why:* RP's core loop; Tensile's blocks already ramp volume but blindly. Evidence: volume–hypertrophy dose response (Schoenfeld et al. 2017; Pelland et al. 2024 meta-regression); feedback-driven autoregulated volume is reasonable but **weakly tested**, so label it as such in the UI. *Where:* `FinishWorkoutSheet`, AppCore `VolumeProgression` with tests. **M.**

**10. Short technique videos for the planner's core exercises.** *What:* silent 3–5 s looping clips (front and side) for the ~60 exercises the planner uses most, recorded for Tensile or licensed (e.g. Hyperhuman's exercise video API; check terms), cached offline; keep YouTube search as a fallback. *Why:* MacroFactor (Nippard), Hevy, Fitbod and Ladder all have videos; it's the most visible gap for newer lifters and builds trust in swaps. *Where:* `ExerciseLibrary`, `ExerciseGuide`. **L** (content work, licence; no GPL/unlicensed sources per CLAUDE.md).

**11. Gym profiles and machine notes.** *What:* more than one gym (home, work, travel), each with its own equipment, chosen at Start or by location; per-exercise notes saved per gym (seat height, pin, handle, which rack). *Why:* MacroFactor ships multiple gym profiles; travel and hotel gyms are a common reason a plan breaks; machine settings are a common Reddit wish. The planner's equipment rule (CLAUDE.md) already supports it per profile. *Where:* `GymProfile` (AppCore), `GymStore`, `MyGymView`, `LocationPicker`. **M.**

**12. Block report and long-term analytics.** *What:* at the end of each training block: e1RM change per main lift, best sets, hard sets per muscle vs the target band, adherence, bodyweight and waist trend, protein average, and what the next block changes. A year view of PRs. *Why:* Whoop added trends and PRs this year; Strong and Hevy users stay for charts; a review at the end of a block is the weekly-review evidence (Michie 2009; Harkin 2016) at block scale. *Where:* new `BlockReview` in AppCore, view off `ThisWeekCard`/`StrengthDetailView`. **M.**

**13. A coach that speaks first, on-device.** *What:* up to three local notifications a day, all optional: morning (today's plan and any change), post-workout (one insight: a PR, a stall, what to do next time), evening (bedtime window, protein gap). Generated with FoundationModels from the app's own numbers, with fixed fallback text when Apple Intelligence is off. *Why:* Whoop (May 2026), Bevel Intelligence V2 and Garmin Active Intelligence all added proactive check-ins; Tensile can match them with nothing leaving the phone. Limit frequency and back off when ignored (Nahum-Shani 2018). *Where:* `Services/Coach`, `Reminders`. **M.**

**14. Onboarding that ends with a ready first workout.** *What:* goal, days per week and session length, gym type (pick a preset: commercial gym, home with barbell, dumbbells only), and two or three anchor lifts (or "new to this"), then show week one with Start. Health access and score explanations come after. *Why:* Hevy Trainer's main appeal is questions → complete program with starting weights; Fitbod's generic first weeks are a known complaint. *Where:* `OnboardingView`, `GoalsView`, `MyGymView` presets, `WorkoutPlanner`. **M.**

### Considered, not recommended now

- **Social feed, followers, leaderboards, badges.** Hevy's feed works for Hevy, but the evidence for gamification is weak and fades (Mazeas 2022), and it adds bloat. The share card (rec. 6) covers most of the demand.
- **Named program library (5/3/1, GZCLP, nSuns).** Boostcamp and Liftosaur own this; Tensile's value is a plan fitted to recovery and equipment. If users ask, offer two or three as *block styles* inside the planner rather than a library.
- **Camera form checking, machine photo scanning.** Unvalidated, and photos would need on-device models only; revisit when Apple's Vision tools make it reliable.
- **Spoken in-workout coaching.** Apple's Workout Buddy already does this for strength on watchOS 26/27 with AirPods; Tensile writes the HKWorkout, so users can run both.

## 4. Things Tensile already does better

- **Plate-exact planning from the user's own gym**, including dumbbell sets and machine stacks, built into the plan rather than a separate calculator (Hevy, Strong and MacroFactor calculate; only Alpha Progression and MacroFactor use available weights for recommendations).
- **Lifting counted properly in strain and recovery** (max of HR load and session RPE), which fixes the Whoop complaint about wrist HR during lifting, plus a muscle-level recovery map.
- **One app for recovery-aware lifting and health.** Whoop, Bevel and Athlytic have shallow lifting; Hevy, Strong and MacroFactor Workouts have no recovery. Juggernaut does readiness for powerlifting at ~$35/month.
- **Pain log that changes exercise selection**, set kinds, abs finisher, easy cardio scheduled around lifting, adaptive week that moves missed sessions: no single competitor has all of these.
- **Private, on-device coach** that returns startable workouts from the user's real gym. Whoop, Bevel, Oura and Garmin coaches run in the cloud and most need a subscription; Bevel's has invented exercises.
- **Evidence labels and honest uncertainty** (±15–25% on calories, ranges on lift goals and body age), while competitors present app-made numbers as precise.
- **Health depth for a lifter:** labs, home BP protocol, vitamins against EARs, supplements with graded evidence, medications with known effects on HR/HRV/sleep, Cronometer intake vs. measured maintenance. Apple's iOS 27 Health app overlaps here; Tensile's edge is linking it to training.
- **Writes the effort score to the HKWorkout**, so Apple's Training Load counts Tensile's lifting.
- **No paywall.**
